## Core argument

### The work has to exist before you can talk about it

The argument for building in public is not about clout. It is about forcing the work to exist. If you cannot show the repo, you probably do not have the repo. If you cannot show the dashboard, the dashboard probably does not exist. If you cannot show the broken version, you probably skipped the part where the work was actually hard.

Building in public is a discipline. It is the commitment to make the work visible at every stage, including the stages where it is ugly and incomplete. That commitment is what separates the people who ship from the people who talk about shipping.

### AURABLOX and shipping ugly early

AURABLOX is the record of the work being done in public. The name is on the site, on the repos, on the dashboards. The work is not hidden behind a pitch deck. It is visible in the form of the thing itself — the code, the architecture, the agent configurations, the days of output.

The ugly early version is not hidden. It is the first draft of the public record.

This is not vanity. It is accountability. When the work is public, the standard is public. The human cannot pretend the work is further along than it is. The work is what it is, and the public record is the proof.

### GitHub as curriculum, portfolio, and classroom

The repos are not just storage. They are curriculum. They are the things a student or a collaborator or a reviewer can actually look at. OpenTutor and the other public projects are classrooms in the sense that they show how the work is done, not just what the work produced.

The stack — React, Firebase, Gemini, whatever is in the repo — matters because it is the evidence of the method. The pitch is cheap. The stack is expensive. The pitch can be written by anyone. The stack is the record of the decisions, the trade-offs, the implementation.

Show the stack. The pitch will follow.

## Working example / proof case

The operator has an idea for a project. The operator creates a repo. The operator commits the first version, which is ugly and incomplete. The operator shows the repo. The operator shows the broken version. The operator shows the stack.

The operator is building in public. The work is visible. The standard is public. The operator cannot pretend the work is further along than it is.

The operator ships the project. The project is visible. The project is evidence.

## What can go wrong

- **Failure mode:** The operator talks about the project without showing the work, and the project does not exist.
- **Why it happens:** The operator is excited about the idea. The operator wants to talk about the vision. The operator does not want to show the ugly early version.
- **How to detect it:** The operator is talking about the project without showing the repo. The operator is talking about the vision without showing the broken version. The operator is talking about the pitch without showing the stack.
- **How the human responds:** The operator creates the repo. The operator commits the first version. The operator shows the work. The operator builds in public.

## Why this matters in 2026

The agent age makes it easier to generate convincing talk. It does not make it easier to generate convincing work. The counter to the talk is the public record — the repos, the dashboards, the broken versions, the shipped things.

Building in public is the way to prove that the work exists. In a noisy market, the proof is the advantage.

## Counterargument / skeptical reader

A critic would say that building in public exposes the operator to competitors, critics, and embarrassment, and that some work should be done in private. That is true for some kinds of work. For the work the operator is doing — building, reviewing, directing — the public record is the proof. The operator should build in public when the proof matters.

The critic would also say that building in public is a marketing strategy disguised as a discipline. That is a fair concern. Building in public can be both. The book's position is that it is first a discipline, and second a marketing strategy. The discipline is what matters. The marketing is a side effect.

## Operator rule

Show the repo. Show the broken version. Show the stack. The work has to exist before you can talk about it.

## Measurable test

Can the operator show the repo? Can the operator show the broken version? Can the operator show the stack? Can the operator explain what the work is and how it works? If the answer to any of those is no, the operator is talking about a project that does not exist.
